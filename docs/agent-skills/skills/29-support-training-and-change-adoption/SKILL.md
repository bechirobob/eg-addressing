# Skill 29 — Support, Training, and Change Adoption

## Use when

Use for helpdesk workflows, operator support, user onboarding, training materials, release communication, SOPs, field rollout, feedback, adoption metrics, or operational change.

## Objective

Ensure people and institutions can use, support, and govern the platform safely—not merely that the software works in development.

## Procedure

1. Identify affected roles, institutions, regions, languages, devices, connectivity, literacy/accessibility needs, and support hours.
2. Define the operational change:
   - current workflow;
   - future workflow;
   - new authority/responsibility;
   - data/process migration;
   - user impact;
   - support impact;
   - rollout wave.
3. Create role-specific onboarding and training using safe fixtures and the exact application version.
4. Distinguish simulation/training from official operations and publication.
5. Create accessible Spanish and English materials:
   - quick starts;
   - task guides;
   - supervisor/admin guides;
   - troubleshooting;
   - privacy/security guidance;
   - incident/escalation paths.
6. Define helpdesk case categories, severity, ownership, SLA, evidence collection, privacy, escalation, and closure.
7. Equip support staff with public-safe and operator-safe diagnostic views without granting excess authority.
8. Define release notes, change notices, downtime/maintenance communication, known limitations, and rollback communication.
9. Run training/pilot exercises and observe completion, errors, excessive clicks, misunderstanding, support demand, and workflow workarounds.
10. Capture feedback and classify it as defect, usability, training, policy, data-quality, infrastructure, or architecture issue.
11. Define adoption metrics: active users, task completion, error/recapture, support cases, training completion, region/institution coverage, and satisfaction—without invasive analytics.
12. Update product backlog, runbooks, training, and standards from operational lessons.

## Required evidence

- Role/institution impact assessment
- Current/future workflow comparison
- Bilingual accessible training artifacts
- Training environment/fixture boundary
- Helpdesk taxonomy and escalation matrix
- Pilot/training exercise results
- Release/change communication
- Adoption and support metrics
- Feedback-to-backlog traceability
- Known limitations and operational owner

## Stop and escalate when

- Users are being onboarded before identity/scope/authority is ready.
- Training uses real personal/evidence data unnecessarily.
- Support staff require privileges beyond their remit.
- A workflow issue is being hidden as “more training” when the design is defective.
- Official operation is planned without named operational ownership.

## Anti-patterns

- Shipping a feature and treating documentation as optional.
- One generic manual for every role.
- Training screenshots that do not match the deployed version.
- Using production credentials or citizen records in training.
- Measuring adoption only by logins.
- Closing support cases without root-cause classification.
- Expecting field users to improvise around connectivity or device constraints.
